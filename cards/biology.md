# What is diffusion?

Diffusion is the movement of particles from an area of high concentration to an area of low concentration.

# What is osmosis?

Osmosis is the movement of water molecules through a partially permeable membrane, from a dilute solution to a more concentrated one.

# What is active transport?

Active transport moves substances against their concentration gradient.

- It requires energy in the form of ATP.
- It uses carrier proteins in the cell membrane.
- Examples: root hair cells absorbing mineral ions, glucose uptake in the gut.

# What is the function of mitochondria?

Mitochondria are the site of aerobic respiration. They release energy from glucose and store it as ATP.

# What is the word equation for photosynthesis?

carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen

The reaction needs light energy and chlorophyll.

# What are the differences between DNA and RNA?

- **DNA** is double-stranded; **RNA** is single-stranded.
- DNA contains the sugar deoxyribose; RNA contains ribose.
- DNA stores genetic information; RNA carries it to the ribosome.
- DNA uses thymine; RNA uses uracil.

# How is the cell membrane structured?

The cell membrane is a phospholipid bilayer. Hydrophilic heads face outwards, hydrophobic tails face inwards, and proteins are embedded in the layer. It is partially permeable, so it controls which substances enter and leave the cell.

# What are enzymes and how do they work?

Enzymes are biological catalysts made of protein.

They speed up reactions by lowering the activation energy. The substrate fits into the enzyme's active site, forming an enzyme–substrate complex. Enzymes are specific: each enzyme works on one substrate. High temperature or the wrong pH denatures the enzyme by changing the shape of the active site.

# What are the stages of mitosis?

1. Prophase — chromosomes condense and the nuclear envelope breaks down.
2. Metaphase — chromosomes line up along the middle of the cell.
3. Anaphase — chromatids are pulled to opposite poles.
4. Telophase — two new nuclei form.
5. Cytokinesis — the cytoplasm divides.

# Why is meiosis important?

Meiosis produces gametes with half the normal number of chromosomes. This keeps the chromosome number constant across generations and creates genetic variation through crossing over and independent assortment.

# What is homeostasis?

Homeostasis is the maintenance of a stable internal environment despite changes in the external environment.

# What are the main components of blood?

- Red blood cells — carry oxygen using haemoglobin.
- White blood cells — defend against pathogens.
- Platelets — help blood to clot.
- Plasma — carries dissolved substances, hormones and waste.

# How does natural selection work?

Individuals in a population vary. Some variants are better suited to the environment, so they survive and reproduce more successfully. Their advantageous alleles are passed to offspring. Over many generations the advantageous traits become more common in the population. This gradual change in allele frequencies is evolution by natural selection.

# What is the difference between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells?

- Prokaryotic cells are small, have no nucleus, and have circular DNA.
- Eukaryotic cells are larger, have a nucleus, and have linear DNA.
- Prokaryotes have no membrane-bound organelles; eukaryotes do.
- Bacteria are prokaryotes; plants, animals and fungi are eukaryotes.

# What is the role of ATP?

ATP is the universal energy currency of the cell. It is produced during respiration and releases energy when it is hydrolysed to ADP and phosphate. That energy drives processes such as active transport, muscle contraction and protein synthesis.
